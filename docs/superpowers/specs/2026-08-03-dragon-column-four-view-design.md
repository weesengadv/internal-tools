# Dragon Column Four-View Design Specification

## Goal

Create a high-resolution four-view orthographic concept sheet for a traditional Chinese stone dragon column. The finished concept must be suitable as a visual reference for later 3D modeling. It is not itself the printable STL/OBJ deliverable.

## Approved Base Structure

- Use the current V9 Blender structural model as the authoritative spatial base.
- Finished overall height: exactly 11 ft.
- Cylindrical shaft diameter: exactly 2 ft.
- Preserve the V9 bold, organically irregular coil rhythm rather than a mechanical constant-pitch helix.
- Preserve one upper shoulder pair and one lower hip pair of legs.
- The left and right roots in each pair must be slightly staggered along the torso so they do not form a projected `+` shape.
- Each pair uses an alternating raised/lowered pose while remaining close to the pillar surface.

## Non-Negotiable Dragon Topology

- The sculpture contains exactly one dragon.
- The dragon has exactly one head, one continuous neck and torso, one tail, and four legs.
- The body must remain continuously traceable from head through neck and torso to the tail tip.
- When the body passes behind the cylindrical pillar, it may disappear only through normal occlusion. It must reappear in the geometrically correct location in the adjacent orthographic view.
- The body must never break, branch into a false tail, dissolve into cloud ornament, or become disconnected decorative fragments.
- Each leg must visibly grow from an anatomically legible shoulder or hip root on the torso.
- Every foot has four claws.

## Dragon Character Reference

Use `codex-clipboard-abdad8f8-55b7-4358-a88e-316c4049c454.png` as the approved character reference for:

- Long projecting muzzle.
- Open mouth with visible teeth and fangs.
- Fierce but dignified expression.
- Paired swept-back horns.
- Flame-shaped brows, cheek fins, mane, and dorsal fins.
- Long whiskers, thickened enough to remain visible and structurally plausible.
- Layered outer body scales.
- Horizontal belly armor plates.
- A small flame-shaped tail fin at the continuous tail tip.

Translate these features directly into grey stone carving. Do not use the reference image's bronze color.

## Carving Direction

Use a dense traditional restoration style based on the original photographed stone dragon pillar.

Relief hierarchy, from highest to lowest:

1. Dragon head: strongest projection and deepest carving.
2. Continuous dragon body: approximately two-thirds embedded into the pillar surface while remaining clearly readable.
3. Legs and four-clawed feet: below the head relief but clearly above background ornament.
4. Clouds, flames, and scrolling foliage: dense shallow relief filling negative space without covering the dragon silhouette, limb roots, body direction, or tail connection.

The rounded layered cap and faceted traditional pedestal remain consistent with the original stone pillar reference.

## Four-View Output

- Produce exactly four full-height orthographic views in this order: front, right, back, left.
- All views show the same 360-degree design at exact 90-degree rotations.
- All columns share identical scale, height, diameter, vertical alignment, cap, pedestal, material, and lighting.
- Each dragon feature leaving the edge of one view must continue at the corresponding edge of the next view.
- Show every pillar in full from top cap to pedestal without cropping or overlap.
- Use a neutral light-grey seamless studio background and soft, uniform lighting.
- Do not include text, labels, dimensions, arrows, borders, watermarks, people, workshop objects, or perspective camera distortion.
- Deliver a high-resolution landscape PNG suitable for close visual inspection by a 3D modeler.

## Source Priority

When references conflict, use this priority order:

1. This approved specification for topology and hard constraints.
2. V9 Blender model for body path, dimensions, and leg locations.
3. The approved dragon character image for head, horns, mane, scales, expression, and claws.
4. The original photographed stone pillar for material, ornament density, cap, pedestal, and traditional carving language.

## Acceptance Criteria

- One continuous dragon can be traced across all four views from head to tail.
- There are exactly four anatomically connected legs, arranged as opposed and slightly staggered upper/lower pairs.
- Each foot visibly carries four claws.
- Coil spacing is intentionally irregular and organic.
- The body remains shallow enough to read as integrated column relief, not a rope placed on top.
- Dense background decoration remains visually subordinate to the dragon.
- Dragon character matches the approved long-muzzled, horned, flame-maned reference.
- All four views are orthographic, equally scaled, and mutually consistent.
- Final canvas contains no text or unrelated visual elements.
